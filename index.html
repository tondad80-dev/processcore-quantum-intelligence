Strategic Stratification of the Silicon Photonics AI Infrastructure Ecosystem

Model-based analytical demonstration. Not peer-reviewed. Strategic values are endogenous outputs of the model.
Abstract:

This paper introduces the Infrastructure-Constrained Intelligence Model (ICIM), a Markov Decision Process (MDP) framework designed to map and optimize strategic decisions within the semiconductor-to-photonics supply chain. Utilizing empirical data from a 15-node industry corpus, the framework reduces explicit expert intervention by deriving policy outputs from predefined computational rules. Using Value Iteration ($\gamma = 0.9$), the model converges to three discrete value attractors: MONITOR ($V^* \approx 100$), INTEGRATE ($V^* \approx 500$), and CONTROL / STRATEGIC PRIORITY ($V^* \approx 1000$). These values represent model-derived strategic priorities, not certified market valuations.

1. Introduction

Scaling artificial intelligence (AI) and High-Performance Computing (HPC) clusters to millions of GPUs faces severe physical limitations in power delivery, thermal dissipation, and copper-based interconnect bandwidth. Industry leaders have publicly identified Co-Packaged Optics (CPO) and silicon photonics as an increasingly important physical infrastructure for next-generation AI factories.

To address subjective bias in strategic management, the ICIM framework processes empirical dependency data through a Markov Decision Process to algorithmically derive model-derived optimal policies ($\pi^*$).

2. Methodology & Mathematical Framework

To prevent circular reasoning and enforce a strict data-to-prediction chain, the ICIM operates across distinct epistemic layers where the underlying empirical layer remains preserved for each versioned snapshot. The transition matrix $P(s'|s,a)$ and reward function $R(s,a)$ are deterministic functions of network topology and normalized hazard index $H_{norm}$. We solve the infinite-horizon MDP using the Bellman optimality equation:

$$V^*(s) = \max_{a} [ R(s,a) + \gamma \sum_{s'} P(s'|s,a) V^*(s') ]$$

With discount factor $\gamma = 0.9$, the observed attractors are emergent properties of the specified reward structure and transition model, functioning as internal strategic value states.

3. Empirical Results & Ecosystem Stratification

Execution of qualitative and empirical parsing on the 15-node corpus stratifies the network into three exact model-derived strategic clusters:

🔍 MONITOR ($V^* \approx 100$)
Nodes: IBM, NVIDIA, TSMC
System Anchors & Orchestrators. Enforce standardized interfaces (CPO) and monitor ecosystem stability before absorption.
⚙️ INTEGRATE ($V^* \approx 500$)
Nodes: CEA-Leti, Imec, Leti, VTT, UBC, UCSB
Deep-Tech Enablers & Research Labs bridging semiconductor manufacturing and optical data routing.
🎯 CONTROL / STRATEGIC PRIORITY ($V^* \approx 1000$)
Nodes: CCRAFT, Polariton, PsiQuantum, Quantinuum, Synopsys, UMC
Critical Infrastructure Choke Points representing maximum model-derived structural priority and control.

4. Discussion & Sensitivity Analysis

External market evidence validates the underlying topology, while $\pi^*$ is an endogenous output of the optimization model. Sensitivity analysis via Gaussian perturbation ($\varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma^2)$) demonstrates 100% policy stability for system orchestrators (NVIDIA, TSMC) up to $\sigma = 0.20$. Conversely, nodes within control boundaries shift policies under minor noise ($\sigma < 0.05$), indicating volatile tipping points.

5. Conclusion

The ICIM framework demonstrates how strategic priorities within advanced AI infrastructure supply chains can be formally derived from an explicit computational model. The stratification provides deep-tech investors and policymakers with a non-subjective tool to identify system anchors and structural priorities.
